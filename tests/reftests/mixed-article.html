How a page becomes pixels

October 2026 · 4 min read

Every web page starts as a stream of bytes. The parser turns those bytes into tokens, and the tree builder assembles the tokens into a document object model that mirrors the structure of the markup.

Style rules are then matched against every element. The cascade resolves conflicts between rules using origin, importance, specificity and source order, and inheritance fills in whatever is left.

Layout is where boxes finally get their sizes and positions.

The painter walks the laid-out boxes in stacking order and records drawing commands, which the rasterizer then turns into anti-aliased pixels.
